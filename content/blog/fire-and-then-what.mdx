Yesterday, I had Korean BBQ for dinner with my ex-boss. Somewhere between flipping the meat on the grill, I mentioned that life is so boring.

![Grilled meat being picked up with chopsticks from a Korean barbecue grill](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1548150914-c9f19106dbf6?w=1600&h=900&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)

*Photo by [Clint Bustrillos](https://unsplash.com/photos/person-picking-grilled-meat-from-grill-using-sticks-UE6KHjZDAUY) on Unsplash*

Then he asked me, "What do you want to do?"

I said, "I need to spend a boring life like this for about 5 more years, then I can retire."

He suddenly asked, "What will you do after you retire?"

That question sank into my mind. The deepest part of me was triggered.

## FIRE Lights Me 🔥

FIRE stands for Financial Independence, Retire Early. The idea is simple: live the cheapest life you can, invest the rest, and retirement comes very early. That's what I wanted.

I first found FIRE through a YouTube channel, and I was hooked.

My professor once said that when he was a student, he wanted to know how an operating system works. So he dug into its source code and read it until he understood it. I think I'm that type too (please let me borrow his words). When I want to know a subject, I want to know it deeply. I want to deep dive into how it works.

FIRE became that subject. I tracked every transaction I made, and I ran the numbers again and again until I knew my FIRE date by heart.

Then I lived it, as cheap as possible. Walk 1-2 km to grab some food instead of ordering through a delivery app. Take the bus because it's cheaper than a taxi. Walk back home instead of calling a Grab.

![Coins falling into a white piggy bank](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1768839721719-c5ed97c1fd58?w=1600&h=900&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)

*Photo by [Sasun Bughdaryan](https://unsplash.com/photos/coins-falling-into-a-white-piggy-bank-eE4uRsnNCsI) on Unsplash*

In my head, retiring meant waking up with nothing to do, reading some books, watching whatever I want, and going anywhere I like without a plan or any worries about money. I believed this was the only way to get there early. Yes, live like this for 5 years.

## Am I Doing It Wrong? 🤔

Since my first day working at a company, I have put every single penny into investments. My thinking was, "If I don't have any desire, any greed for things while I'm young, I'll grow my portfolio faster."

I had no life. I had a fixed routine: wake up early every morning, eat some chicken BBQ in front of my house, and stay home. This cycle repeated itself for many years, especially during COVID-19.

This mindset dragged me down in many things. It held my decisions and made me not want to step out of my comfort zone. Even in a relationship, this extreme FIRE behavior followed me. I wanted to live in a cheap apartment so I could have more money to invest. When I travel, I need to be very cheap with every penny.

I remember standing by the river in Nakhon Ratchasima. There were canoes for rent, only a couple hundred baht. I should have rented one and paddled along the river. I was at that place, at that moment.

![A person in a canoe paddling down a river](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1691197672191-d07124391453?w=1600&h=900&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)

No, I didn't. We just drove back home. "This is the right way. Let's keep doing this."

… until I realized something. 🤔

## Two Triggers

The realization came from two conversations: one with an ex-colleague, and one with my ex-boss.

First, my ex-colleague asked, "What will you do after you have all that money?"

I once made a profit from selling a fund, and I promised myself I would spend it on the things I want. A new game, gadgets, accessories, clothes, running shoes. Turns out, that money stayed in the bank for a couple of months without a single penny leaving. I realized at that moment: this is not my desire. I don't want these things.

That emptiness anchored itself in my mind. What about the next 10 years? You'll have a huge investment portfolio, and what will you spend it on?

Second, my ex-boss said, "Okay, now you're saving every penny. What will you do after you retire?" Then he answered it himself. "You'll eventually get bored, just like now."

Exactly. That's what I answered too 😆.

He said, "Go have a life. Spend some of your money. Don't keep it and let it hold you down like this." He knows me. My nature is to explore new things, go to new places, meet new people.

This was the second time that the emptiness anchored deep in my mind floated up to the surface. That is not usual for me. So I stopped to think, and I'm writing this blog.

## What's Next?

Normally I would write this in my diary to remind myself. But I hope this post finds you at some stage of your life.

Don't get me wrong. FIRE is not the problem. Saving and investing gave me a safety net and calm when things go wrong at work. I'm not going to stop. The problem was the "cheapest possible" part, when saving stopped being a tool and became my whole life.

### Strike a Balance

> "มัชฌิมาปฏิปทา" or "Strike a balance"

This is the teaching from the Buddha that still reminds me. He tortured himself until he was only skin and bone. Then he found the middle way.

I think I've done enough of the torture part. Now it's time to put a balance variable into the equation.

![A stack of smooth dark stones balanced on a beach](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1536623975707-c4b3b2af565d?w=1600&h=900&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)

*Photo by [Colton Sturgeon](https://unsplash.com/photos/stack-of-smooth-stones-on-beach-6KkYYqTEDwQ) on Unsplash*

So here is my new equation: the investing keeps going, and the rest is for living.

I promise myself that from this moment on, I will spend some money to try new experiences. Go to places I have never been. Meet more people. Enjoy the other part of life.

The first one is already on my list. I'm going back to Nakhon Ratchasima, and this time I'm renting the canoe.
